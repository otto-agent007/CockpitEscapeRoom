# Pop T at native stage resolution

## Purpose

Pop T is the comedy lead of the 53-second intro and he currently reads as a cheap sprite pasted
onto expensive artwork. After this work he is drawn at the resolution his on-screen size actually
demands, on the same pixel grid as everything else in the frame, so the intro reads as one picture
instead of a character composited over a painting.

## Discoveries

**2026-08-17 — the premise of this plan was partly wrong.** The Purpose above says Pop T should be
drawn "on the same pixel grid as everything else in the frame". Measured against the shipped
plates: there is no such grid. The five backgrounds under `public/images/intro/tmb2/backgrounds/`
are 1586x992 paintings carrying 118k-208k distinct colours with ~47-54% single-pixel runs. The
orphan-pixel ceiling the v2 contract gates on is a pixel-art-specific measure that those plates
fail at **92.9%**; full-colour Pop T scores **78.6%**, i.e. below the artwork he stands in front
of, while the quantised build scores 0.7%. Enforcing a 14-colour palette on the character alone
was producing the very defect this plan exists to remove — a character composited over a painting.

What the constraint cost, measured on the Wave 0 anchor at the 19x reduction: the mouth was
deleted outright (drawn as a 6px skin-toned crease, it lost every modal vote), the belt was
fragmented by `#F1EFF0` pixels punched through the near-black band where cells straddled the shirt
hem, and both thumbs flattened into the hand. All three survive intact on the full-colour route.
Evidence: `preview-renders/tmb2-intro-overhaul/popt-v2-belt-hands-detail.png` and
`popt-v2-look-comparison.png` (both composited over `duffel-terminal` at stage scale).

The intermediate option — smooth downsample then re-quantise to a larger adaptive palette — was
tested and is worse, not a compromise: 7.5% orphan at 16 colours rising to 16.5% at 32, because
chopping anti-aliased gradients without dithering manufactures banding speckle.

**The integer-scaling invariant is untouched.** The sprite is still pre-rendered at exactly its
on-stage size and blitted 1:1 into the 320x224 stage, which scales to screen by an integer. Plan
0030's work stands; only the palette constraint is retired.

Measured 2026-08-16 against the shipped build. Each figure is reproducible from the repository.

- **Pop T was drawn at a fractional scale.** The sheets are 256×256 with a 2 px art grid, drawn at
  `scale` 1.08–1.18 depending on call site (1.12 by default) onto the 320×224 stage. One art pixel
  therefore covered 2.24 stage pixels. A horizontal run-length census of `duffel-pull-01` at the
  runtime scale: 44.8% of runs came out an odd number of pixels wide — 2 px 34.9%, 3 px 11.1%. The
  silhouette stepped raggedly against a background drawn 1:1. At scale 1 the same census is 0.0%
  odd.

- **He is under-resolved for the size he plays at.** The character is 37×46 art pixels and occupies
  ~41% of stage height. The key mascot in the same frames is 84×75 art pixels at a *smaller*
  on-stage size, because it is drawn at scale 0.38 and supersamples. Pop T resolves 0.45 art pixels
  per stage pixel; the key resolves 1.3. That is roughly a 5× detail gap between two characters
  standing next to each other, and it is why one reads as polished and the other as blocky.

- **The v1 cell size was a tool default, not a derivation.** `sizes.canonical` 128×128 and pivot
  (64,112) are the Pixel Sprite Animation Pipeline's documented defaults. Nothing in the pipeline
  relates them to the 320×224 stage, and its validation checks palette, pivot, baseline, and
  landmark drift — all of which passed green — while never compositing the sprite on the stage at
  its real size. A pipeline that validates a sprite only in isolation cannot catch a 2.24× scale.
  The character ended up filling 46 of the cell's 128 rows and was then enlarged at runtime.

- **The frames carry snap speckle.** 13.1% of Pop T's opaque pixels are orphans — no 4-neighbour of
  the same colour — versus 8.5% for the key mascot. Visible at 22× as single stray pixels off the
  arm and lavender noise inside the flat white shirt. This is the signature of quantising a
  generated image rather than authoring pixel art.

- **The punch camera resamples the whole stage on 93.5% of the intro.** Sampling all 1327 frames at
  25 Hz: camera zoom is exactly 1 on 86 frames (6.5%), 1.00–1.05 on 268, 1.05–1.15 on 932,
  1.15–1.25 on 37, and ≥1.25 on 4. The 0028 camera is a fractional world transform applied around
  every world command, so background, props, and sprites are all point-sampled off-grid for almost
  the entire runtime. Reduced motion is identity and is unaffected. **This is the single largest
  contributor to the frame looking soft, it is not an asset problem, and correcting Pop T will not
  fix it.** *Resolved 2026-08-17 by `plans/0030-pixel-safe-punch-camera.md`: the nine held scene
  baselines were retired, so the intro is now pixel-exact on 93.3% of frames and only the punch
  envelopes lift the zoom. That change is why this contract's standing height moved to 104.*

- **Eight call sites rotate Pop T at runtime** (slide tilt, glide sway, bull tumble, lunge sag, exit
  roll). Any non-zero rotation resamples a pixel sprite; Genesis hardware could not rotate sprites
  at all. Not changed here. The v2 frame budget adds authored tilt frames to `pilot-glide` and
  `bull-spin` so the rotation can be retired later without losing the motion.

- **The stage-to-screen scale is already correct.** `computeIntroStagePlacement` floors it to a
  whole number; measured 4× at a 1440×900 viewport with a 320×224 backing store. That layer was
  never the problem.

## Decision log

- **2026-08-16 — Re-author the asset, not the tooling.** Owner decision. GameDevStuff is left
  untouched; its Pixel Snapper may still be used to process frames, but no repair work goes into it.
- **2026-08-16 — Owner generates frames via ChatGPT Image.** Continues the 2026-08-15 decision for
  the acting packs. Consequence: the deliverable on this side is a contract plus a prompt pack plus
  a validator, and the critical path runs through the owner.
- **2026-08-16 — Target one art pixel per stage pixel, character 92 px tall.** 92 is the v1 idle
  height doubled exactly, which is also what the corrected runtime already renders from the v1
  frames at scale 1. Choosing it means the staging is tuned once and does not move again when the
  new frames land. Consequence: Pop T is ~12% shorter on stage than the ragged 105 px he was, and
  gains headroom in the close-ups.
- **2026-08-16 — Keep pivot (64,112) and baseline 111.** Preserves every choreographed y position
  from 0022 and 0028 and keeps continuity with the approved v1 contract.
- **2026-08-16 — Keep the 14-colour palette exactly.** Identity preservation. One free slot remains
  in the 16-colour budget if the redraw needs one more shading step.
- **2026-08-16 — Frame budget 40 → 55.** The acting gap named in 0028 is real; the redraw is the
  cheapest moment to close part of it, since every frame is being regenerated anyway.
- **2026-08-17 — Standing height raised 92 → 104 and the pivot moved to (64,120).** Consequence of
  the owner approving plan 0030 on 2026-08-16. With the held camera zoom retired there is no
  magnification left to borrow, so the art must carry the size. 104 is the tallest standing pose
  the 128 cell can hold while leaving headroom for raised arms, and it lands ~9% under the 0028
  average apparent size. The pivot moved down 8 rows inside the cell purely to buy that
  headroom; it does not move him on stage. The contract records a `dependsOn` block so the two
  number sets are never mixed.
- **2026-08-16 — Leave the fractional punch-camera zoom alone.** *(superseded 2026-08-16 — the
  owner asked for it to be scoped, then approved it; see `plans/0030-pixel-safe-punch-camera.md`.)* Out of the scope the owner set,
  and it changes approved choreography. Documented above as the largest remaining defect.

### 2026-08-16 gate

The validator is proven in both directions, because a gate that cannot fail is not a gate.

- **Negative control — it rejects the art we have.**
  `python3 tools/assets/check-popt-frames.py public/images/intro/tmb2/popt --grid-step 2`
  exits 3 with 30 failures over 24 frames: all 24 frames exceed the orphan-pixel ceiling
  (9.7%–16.6% against a 6% limit) and 6 clips are short of their v2 frame count. Palette,
  envelope, baseline, and silhouette checks pass on v1, which is correct — v1 is palette-clean

  the v1 duffel-pull poses, doubled to the v2 size, de-speckled by majority-neighbour fill, and
  placed on the baseline: `--grid-step 1` exits 0 with 0 failures. This only proves the gate is
  passable; the synthetic frames are a control, are not committed, and are not art.
- The gate is deliberately **not** wired into `npm run assets:check` yet — it would fail the
  currently shipped frames. It runs per wave until the v2 set is complete, then joins the suite.
